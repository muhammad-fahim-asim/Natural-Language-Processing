MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING
between
THE STATE OF TEXAS
and
THE FREE AND SOVEREIGN STATE OF
COAHUILA DE ZARAGOZA
MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING made by and between the State of Texas and the Free
and Sovereign State of Coahuila de Zaragoza ("Memorandum"). The State of Texas is referred to
as "Texas"; the Free and Sovereign State of Coahuila de Zaragoza is referred to as "Coahuila";
individually, each party may be referred to as a "State"; and collectively, the parties are referred
to as "States."
WHEREAS, the States have a mutual interest in ensuring that their shared border is secure; and
WHEREAS, Texas anticipates a significant rise in cartel-facilitated smuggling through the use of
unsafe vehicles due to President Biden's decision to end Title 42 expulsions; and
WHEREAS, Texas has implemented enhanced safety inspections of vehicles as they cross
international ports of entry to reduce the number of unsafe vehicles that enter Texas; and
WHEREAS, these inspections- - which have revealed that almost 25% of the commercial vehicles
that crossed into Texas were unsafe have slowed border crossings for commercial vehicles; and
WHEREAS, given the importance of trade between Texas and Coahuila, it is in the best interests
of the States to enter into this Memorandum;
NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved that Texas and Coahuila will reduce the number of unsafe
vehicles approaching the border, and the corresponding need for enhanced safety inspections, by
using their best efforts as follows:
1. The States will work cooperatively to ensure that vehicles crossing an international port of
entry meet the safety standards applicable in each State.
2. The States will work cooperatively to reduce the cartels' human trafficking and smuggling
of deadly fentanyl and other drugs across and between ports of entry.
3. The States will work cooperatively to stop the flow of migrants who illegally enter Texas
through Coahuila.
4. Coahuila will enhance border security enforcement measures on the Coahuila side of the
border, both at ports of entry and along the Rio Grande River, to prevent illegal
immigration from Coahuila into Texas by committing to the following:



a. Operate checkpoints already established in different parts of the State in order to
detect the arrival of immigrants before they enter Coahuila. Once migrants are
detected at these checkpoints they will be returned to a designated place in Mexico.
b. At the Municipal level, in both borders, special teams of personnel and motor
equipment will be designated to prevent the crossing of immigrants arriving at the
ports of Ciudad Acuna and/or Piedras Negras, in order to ensure that no migrant
shall cross the border at those locations - including providing resources to respond
to hot spot areas as identified by the Texas Department of Public Safety. This effort
includes using helicopters and all terrain vehicles to patrol the border.
5. The States will work cooperatively to restore the border-crossing inspections process to
allow crossings at a faster pace.
This Memorandum may be modified by mutual consent of the States in writing. Notwithstanding
any other provision in this Memorandum, the Memorandum may be terminated by either State
upon written notice thirty days before the date of termination.
