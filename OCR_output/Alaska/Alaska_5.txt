This Memorandum of Understanding is made on 11th September, 2013 between:
The Department of Natural Resources of the State of Alaska (Alaska DNR) of 550 West 7th
Avenue, Suite 1400 Anchorage, Alaska, 99501-3650
and
Japan Bank for International Cooperation ("JBIC") of 4-1, Ohtemachi l-chome, Chiyoda-ku,
Tokyo 100-8144, Japan, collectively hereinafter referred to as the "Parties".
Recitals:
1. For mutual benefits, the Parties agree to enhance closer economic relationship between the
State of Alaska and Japan and hereby share the view that confirming the Parties' cooperation
through concluding this Memorandum of Understanding is beneficial for both entities.
2. (i)
Alaska has one of the largest portfolios of oil, gas, minerals, renewable resources,
water and land in the world;
(ii) The North Slope of Alaska has estimates of over 200 trillion cubic feet of
conventional gas and 35 trillion cubic feet of known gas reserves in existing North
Slope fields based on recent United States Geological Survey reports;
(iii) Compared to other areas, Alaska is relatively underexplored and a recent study by the
Brookings Institution notes the strong competitive position of a potential, large-scale
Alaska LNG to Asia project in comparison with other global LNG projects;
(iv) Much of the upstream infrastructure is in place and hundreds of millions of dollars
have been spent on critical engineering and environmental regulatory and commercial
work required for a gas project in Alaska;
(v) The State of Alaska has made substantial progress over the past year toward
commercializing Alaska North Slope gas;
(vi) The State of Alaska has undertaken a comprehensive strategy to highlight the
comparative advantages of Alaska LNG to potential investors, buyers, consumers,
and government officials, particularly in Asia;
(vii) According to the United States Geological Surveys, the State of Alaska ranks in the
top ten of the world for occurrences in coal, copper, lead, gold, zinc, and silver in
addition to having over 70 occurrences ofl Rare Earth Elements (REES);
(viii) The State of Alaska is also looking to alternative sources of energy with the goal to
use 50% renewable electrical power by 2025 and thus has supported alternate sources
of renewable energy and has assisted in examining feasibility of projects that utilize



woody biomass, hydropower, wind power and clean coal technologies;
(ix) The State of Alaska regularly engages with public entities and companies, both
domestic and foreign, interested in Alaska's resources to provide information that will
attract international investment and enhance cross-border commerce;
(x) The State of Alaska has expressed interest in providing American and Japanese
companies with business opportunities; and
(xi) Cooperation with JBIC enables DNR to gain a better understanding of the rapidly
changing Japanese and Asian markets and the interests of investors for LNG.
3. JBIC's mission is to contribute to the sound development of the Japanese and international
economy by conducting international finance operation in the following four fields:
(i)
Promoting overseas development and acquisition of strategically important natural
resources to Japan;
(ii) Maintaining and improving the international competitiveness of Japanese industries;
(ili) Promoting overseas projects for conserving the global environment, such as
mitigating global warming; and
(iv) Responding to disruption in the financial order in the international economy.
4. JBIC has expressed interest in contributing and supporting the organizing from an early stage
of projects to be implemented by Japanese companies in the State of Alaska that meet JBIC's
financial policy and requirement.
In light of recognitions mentioned above, the Parties have agreed to the following:
ARTICLE 1:
AGENDA FOR DISCUSSION
The Parties may, at their respective sole discretion, exchange information or views with
respect to the following subjects:
(i)
Potential resource development projects that the State of Alaska wishes to
advance -in particular a large-volume natural gas pipeline and export facilities;
other projects could include areas such as: oil, strategic and critical minerals
(including rare earth elements), iron ore, coal, clean coal technologies, alumina,
woodchip, infrastructure, power (including hydropower), environmental technologies,
water and waste management, and other renewable energy technologies;
(ii) Potential projects in Alaska that the State of Alaska would like JBIC to evaluate for
possible international financial investment;
(iii) The operations and policies of JBIC as they relate to potential Alaska projects; and
(iv) Other items the Parties may wish to discuss in relation to potential opportunities for
cooperation.
ARTICLE 2:
MEETINGS
The Parties will meet from time to time in order to discuss the agenda set forth in Article 1
of this Memorandum of Understanding and mutual collaboration in organizing mission and
events by the Parties.



ARTICLE 3: CONFIDENTIALITY
Subject to statutory requirements of making all records open to the public in accordance
with the applicable laws of the State of Alaska, each of the Parties shall be obliged to keep the
Confidential Information (as defined below) confidential, to the same degree as each Party
manages its own Confidential Information, and shall not disclose to third parties any
Confidential Information without obtaining the prior written approval of the Party disclosing the
Confidential Information (such Party, the "Disclosing Party"). "Confidential Information"
includes all business, financial, scientific, technical, and other information and materials
(whether in writing or otherwise) that is notified by the Disclosing Party to be confidential, but
does not include information that is required to be disclosed in accordance with applicable laws,
or regulations or orders, etc. of courts, or the relevant ministries or administrative agencies, or
any other public entities.
ARTICLE 4: NON-EXCLUSIVITY
This Memorandum of Understanding is nonexclusive. Neither Party is precluded by this
Memorandum of Understanding from engaging in similar or dissimilar memoranda, agreements
or projects with third parties.
ARTICLE 5: TERMINATION
This Memorandum of Understanding shall expire in two years from the date of its signature
by the Parties and shall be renewed for the same period of time every two years when one of the
Parties notifies the other with thirty days prior notice of its decision to renew the same.
Notwithstanding the above, either Party may at any time terminate this Memorandum of
Understanding by giving thirty days prior notice in writing to the other Party. Provided,
however, that the confidentiality obligations set forth in Article 3 shall survive the expiration of
this Memorandum ofUnderstanding for a period not to exceed five years from such expiration.
The anticipated termination of the present Memorandum ofUnderstanding shall not affect if
SO desired by the Parties the conclusion of the actions of cooperation that may have been
formalised during its operation.
ARTICLE 6: OTHERS
It is understood and agreed by the Parties, that this Memorandum of Understanding is
written only to record the Parties intention of strengthening mutual cooperation, and that this
Memorandum of Understanding is in no way to be construed as to entail or imply rights or
obligations (financial or otherwise) of each or any of them, and that neither Party shall be liable
to the other Party under any legal theory for a party's performance under this Memorandum of
Understanding
It is further understood and agreed by the Parties, that neither Party may act or bind the
other Party in any way, nor may either Party represent or lead others to believe that it is in any
way responsible for the acts of the other Party or representing the other Party. This
Memorandum of Understanding does not establish a joint venture or partnership between the
Parties.
Nothing contained in any discussion between the Parties or any information disclosed by
either party as contemplated in this Memorandum of Understanding shall be deemed to
constitute a representation or warranty.



The Parties have caused this Memorandum of Understanding to be executed in Tokyo as of the
date first above written. Each person signing this Memorandum of Understanding hereby
represents and warrants that he or she is authorized to execute and deliver this Memorandum of
Understanding.
