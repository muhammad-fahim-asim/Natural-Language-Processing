MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING
BETWEEN
THE REPUBLIC OF CHINA (TAIWAN) THROUGH THE MINISTRY OF TRANSPORATION
AND COMMUNICATIONS
AND
THE ARKANSAS DEPARTMENT OF FINANCE & ADMINISTRATION
REGARDING THE MUTUAL. RECOGNITION OF
DRIVER'S LICENSES
This Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) is made between the Arkansas Department of Finance &
Administration and the Republic of China (Taiwan) through the Ministry ofTransportation and
Communications, represented by the Taipei Economic & Cultural Office in Houston, for reciprocity in
issuing driver licenses between Taiwan and State of Arkansas. The Arkansas Department of Finance &
Administration (hereinafter the "DFA") and the Ministry of'Transportation and Communications of the
Republic of China (Taiwan) (hereinafter jointly referred to as the "Sides"),
Acknowledging that their respective governments have established comparable knowledge and driving
skills requirements which must be demonstrated by a driver's license applicant under comparable
standards for testing knowledge and driving skills prior to a driver's license being issued; and
Desiring to streamline the exchange of valid non-commercial driver's licenses for an applicant from the
Republic of China (Taiwan) living in Arkansas and an applicant from Arkansas living in the Republic of
China (Taiwan).
The following sets forth the principles and procedures for achieving the aforementioned
objective without creating legal obligation.
DFA and Republic of China (Taiwan) have reached the following understanding:
Section 1
a.
The DFA may allow a person aged 18 or over, lawfully present in the United States and
residing in Arkansas who possesses a valid, unexpired Republic of China (Taiwan)
driver's license (Class B), to exchange that license for an Arkansas noncommercial
(Class D) Operator's License without a vehicle group designation or endorsement. The
applicant will be required to pay all requisite fees and pass an eyesight test, but will not
be required to submit to a driving skills test or knowledge test.
b.
The Republic of China (Taiwan) may allow a person aged 18 or over, residing in the
Republic of China (Taiwan) who possesses a valid, unexpired Arkansas Operator's
License (Class D), to exchange that license for a Class B Ordinary Driver's License after
paying all requisite fees and passing a physical (aptitude) test, without being required to
submit to a driving skills test or knowledge test.
C.
The Sides may require a driving skills test and/or medical documentation attesting to the
physical or mental capabilities of applicants prior to granting licensing in situations
where the Side, carrying out the exchange has reason to believe that the individual is not
qualified to safely operate a motor vehicle. All other legal or policy requirements in
Arkansas and/or the Republic of China (Taiwan) will apply.



d.
Each Side will require all applicants for a driver's license to present proof of identity and
lawful presence as required to comply with the laws and regulations of its country and/or
state, and will apply the same application standards, rules, and regulations in licensing
decisions as it would for any resident of the country and/or state, including the
suspension, revocation or restriction of a license already issued. as well as the
reexamination of a driver previously granted a license.
e.
The DFA may require an applicant to present an International Driving Permit or a
notarized translation issued by a Republic ofChina (Taiwan) Consulate General (also
known as Taipei Economic & Cultural Office in Houston, TECO in Houston) to assist in
the translation of the Republic of China (Taiwan) driver's license. In addition, the DFA
may require an applicant to provide a translation into the English language of any
documents presented as identification or proofof lawful presence.
f.
The Republic of China (Taiwan) may require an applicant to present the certificate of
authentication ofthe Arkansas driver's license issued by Embassy (also known as
American Institute in Taiwan, AIT) ofthe United States of America in Republic ofChina
(Taiwan) or issued by the government of the State of Arkansas.
Section 2
a.
The Sides will provide to each other samples oftheir relevant driver's licenses in
circulation at the date ofthe coming into effect of this MOU and any versions that may
be introduced in the future.
b.
In case of doubts related to the validity or authenticity of a driver's license submitted for
exchange, with the consent of the holder oft the driver's license for release ofthis
information, the Sides may verify with each other that the applicant has a valid driver's
license issued by the respective agency. If consent is withheld, an exchange may not
occur.
C.
The Side requested to verify the validity or authenticity of an applicant's driver's license
will promptly respond to the request for verification.
Section 3
a.
This MOU is subject to the relevant laws, rules, and regulations in force in the Republic
of China (Taiwan) and the State of Arkansas.
b.
Both Sides will cooperate in implementing this MOU.
C.
Both Sides will respect all State and Federal privacy laws with regard to any information
obtained when exchanging a driver's license, including the Driver's Privacy Protection
Act, 18 U.S.C. S 2721, et seq., and the Republic of China (Taiwan) Personal Information
Protection Law.

a.

resolved through discussions between the Sides
C.

d.
This MOU becomes effective when signed by the last signatory hereto and may be
terminated by either Side at will at any time with a thirty (30) day written notice, except
in the case of misuse of data or a security breach, in which case each Side may
immediately terminate the MOU.
