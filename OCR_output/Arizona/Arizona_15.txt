MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING BETWEEN THE STATE OF ARIZONA OF
THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA AND THE MINISTRY OF COMMUNICATIONS
AND TRANSPORT OF THE UNITED MEXICAN STATES ON THE ESTABLISHMENT
OF A JOINT PLANNING COMMITTEE ON THE BINATIONAL TRADE CORRIDOR
CONSISTING OF MEXICO HIGHWAY 15 AND THE PORT OF ENTRY SYSTEM IN
ARIZONA AND THE STATE AND FEDERAL HIGHWAYS THAT LINK THE PORTS TO
THE FEDERAL HIGHWAY SYSTEM
The Ministry of Communications and Transport of the United Mexican States and the
State of Arizona of the United States of America (the State or Arizona), hereinafter
referred to as "the Participants";
WHEREAS:
I. The governments of the State of Arizona and the United Mexican States have a
history of friendship and excellent cooperation, including on issues related
to infrastructure and commerce.
II. The Ports of Entry between the United Mexican States and the United States
of America represent an important economic factor that requires adequate
land connections in order to exchange goods.
III. Ongoing bi-national cooperation between the United States of America and the
United Mexican States to construct and maintain infrastructure at the Ports of
Entry and along important international trade routes is critical for the safe and
efficient flow of people and goods.
IV. On May 2, 2013, President Barack Obama and President Enrique Pena
Nieto agreed to establish a High Level Economic Dialogue, to advance
strategic economic and commercial priorities central to promoting mutual
economic growth, job creation, and competitiveness.
V. Both Presidents reaffirmed the importance of the 21st Century Border
Management Initiative, in which the United States of America and the United
Mexican States agreed to support key projects and initiatives that improve
infrastructure, facilitate the secure flow of legitimate commerce and travel, and
enhance law enforcement cooperation along the border.
VI.
The United Mexican States' National Development Plan 2013-2018 outlines its
main administration goals and policy actions. The "Prosperous Mexico"
section focuses on modernizing, expanding and maintaining the infrastructure
for all modes of transportation as well as improving its connectivity through
the use of strategic criteria and efficiency. This approach will allow for a
transportation infrastructure that promotes economic development at a lower
cost. The "Prosperous Mexico" promotes the development of new
infrastructure that in turn promotes competition, innovation and growth in
strategic sectors, and also assesses the long-term infrastructure needs for
2



economic growth considering regional development, demographic trends, and
international connectivity, among others.
VII. The State of Arizona is committed to fostering global trade and established the
Transportation and Trade Corridor Alliance to promote the development of
transportation infrastructure that facilitates global trade and tourism and
fosters job creation, with a particular focus on trade with the United Mexican
States, Arizona's largest trading partner.
VIII. Mexico Highway 15 is the most important corridor in western United Mexican
States and the primary corridor providing access to Arizona, California and the
inter-mountain west of the United States.
IX. The United States of America and the State of Arizona have made a
significant investment into the corridor's major commercial point of entry into
the United States, the Mariposa Port of Entry (known in Mexico as Nogales II),
totaling more than $250 million in combined investments between the United
States of America federal government and the Arizona Department of
Transportation.
X.
The SCT has also made a significant investment into Highway 15, which
connects central Mexico and the states of Mexico, Jalisco, Nayarit, Sinaloa and
Sonora to the border with Arizona. These improvements include the
construction of bypasses around major cities, such as Guadalajara, Tepic,
Culiacan, Ciudad Obregon and Hermosillo, in order to improve the efficiency of
the corridor and the safety of the traveling public.
XI. Major challenges remain in the corridor, including inadequate infrastructure,
insufficient personnel, bottlenecks, concerns about security, and the need to
modernize the corridor to include technology improvements and intelligent
transportation systems.
Have reached the following understanding:
Paragraph 1
Objective
The Participants intend to create a Joint Planning Committee ("Committee") with
representatives from the SCT, and the State of Arizona. The understanding for the
Committee is the following: the Committee should collaborate to improve the
Binational Corridor that is comprised of Interstate 19 (1-19) in Arizona and Highway 15
(MX-15) in Mexico. This corridor is the primary north-south corridor in Western United
Mexican States and the Western United States of America and critically important to
the overall economic development and trade of both countries. The Committee should
establish a working plan to improve the physical infrastructure and policies that should
enable this corridor to become more efficient.
3



The Committee's working plan for the Corridor should include the identification of
priority projects, identification of and responses to issues causing backups along the
Arizona-Mexico border, ways to make the corridor more efficient for multi-modal
transportation, and technologies that need to be deployed along the corridor to
improve efficiency and reliability.
One critical element of defining the Committee's working plan should be a binational
planning study for the Corridor. The Committee should approve the scope of work for
the binational planning study and will serve to oversee the study and complete its final
contents and recommendations. Among the areas to be considered for inclusion in
the binational planning study are:
1. issues impacting the efficiency and safety of the corridor;
2. priority areas in need of future investment, including options to have full staffing
schemes at the ports of entry;
3. current and potential industry clusters and supply chain relationships along the
corridor;
4. recommendations for the deployment of technology, including intelligent
transportation systems and technologies and systems that can help expedite
safety and security inspections along the corridor;
5. issues impacting railroad connectivity and efficiency along the corridor, including
at the border; and
6. opportunities for the sharing of non-confidential data and information in order to
maximize binational coordination and return on investment on both sides of the
border
The Committee should work to finalize a scope of work for the binational planning
study, identify funding for the study, and oversee the study's completion. As work is
finalized, the Committee should submit to the consideration of the U.S. and Mexican
competent authorities the findings and recommendations arising from the study.
Paragraph 2
Coordination
The Participants intend to maintain continuous coordination and communication to
ensure the efficient and effective implementation of this Memorandum of
Understanding. The Director of the Arizona Department of Transportation and the
Undersecretary of Infrastructure of the SCT shall intend to designate a representative
from each agency to monitor the implementation of this Memorandum of
Understanding.

M



The Participants intend to meet in person or telephonically, as needed, to address
and resolve issues described in Article 1 of this Memorandum of Understanding.
Paragraph 3
Committee Membership
The Committee should be formed at minimum by two representatives each participant.
The Participants by mutual decision may invite other U.S. and Mexican competent
authorities to integrate the Committee.
The Participants could, as well, invite individuals and entities from the private,
academic, research, and other sectors to support this collaboration, provided that they
can directly and meaningfully contribute to the achievement of the objectives of this
Memorandum of Understanding.
Paragraph 4
Financing
The Participants intend to finance their own expenses and activities referred to in
Paragraph 1 with resources allocated in their respective budgets, to the extent that
these resources become available and as permitted by applicable laws and
regulations.
Paragraph 5
Information Sharing
All useful, non-confidential information that supports the objectives in Paragraph 1
may be shared to accomplish the goals of this Memorandum of Understanding, if
shared, pursuant to the lawful authorities of each participating jurisdiction.
Paragraph 6
Intellectual Property
If, as a result of the activities developed in accordance with Paragraph 1 of this
Memorandum of Understanding, products of commercial or intellectual value are
derived, the products shall be governed by the corresponding laws applicable to each
of the Participants, as well as the related international conventions legally binding to
the United Mexican States and the United States of America.

Paragraph 7
Labor Relationships
The personnel assigned by each of the Participants to the execution of the
cooperation activities under this Memorandum of Understanding, should continue
under the direction and dependence of the institution to which they pertain, and no
labor relationships should be created with the other Participants which in no case
should be considered as a substitute or contract employee, in accordance with the
applicable national legislation.
Paragraph 8
Interpretation and Modification
Any matters concerning the application or interpretation of this Memorandum of
Understanding, should be settled through consultation between the Participants.
The Memorandum of Understanding can be modified by mutual decision of the
Participants in writing, specifying the date of the entry into force of any such
modifications.
Any differences of interpretation or management of implementation of this
Memorandum of Understanding will be resolved solely by mutual agreement of the
Parties.
Paragraph 9
Duration and Termination
Termination of this Memorandum of Understanding can be made by either of the
Parties, through written communication directed to the other Participant thirty (30)
calendar days in advance of the termination.
Termination of this Memorandum of Understanding should not affect the completion
of cooperation activities initiated while it was in force, unless otherwise jointly decided
in writing by the Parties.
Paragraph 10
Enforceability
The Parties acknowledge that this Memorandum of Understanding is only intended to
provide for cooperation and a framework for collaboration in order to advance the
tasks as outlined in Paragraph 1 of this Memorandum between the Participants and
does not create any legally binding rights or obligations.
