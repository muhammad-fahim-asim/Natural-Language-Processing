MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING (MOU) EXECUTED BY THE
ARIZONA - MEXICO COMMISSION AND THE COMISION SONORA
ARIZONA FOR THE PURPOSE OF MUTUALLY RECOGNIZING THE
CONDITIONS AND REQUIREMENTS TO EXECUTE THE "2015: ARIZONA
AND SONORA'S NEW BORDER" PLAN REGARDING ARIZONA AND
SONORA'S BORDER INFRASTRUCTURE TIMELINE IN ACCORDANCE WITH
THE FOLLOWING:
WHEREAS, Arizona's border ports of entry are critical components of the
economic and physical security of both states; and
WHEREAS, studies by the University of Arizona have indicated that Arizona has
lost a substantial portion of cross-border trade for at least five years; and
WHEREAS, Customs and Border Protection (CBP) data confirms that between
2002 and 2007 car traffic dropped by 17% (one million fewer cars) and car
passenger traffic decreased by 20% (four million fewer car passengers) and that
pedestrian traffic increased by 25% (more than two million more pedestrians)
during that same time period; and
WHEREAS, commercial traffic has grown by 17% (more than 55,000 more
trucks) over the same time frame and is expected to double or? triple in the next
15 to 20 years; and
WHEREAS, Arizona's and Sonora's ports of entry are on average more than 30
years old and are extremely overburdened and outdated; and
WHEREAS, the federal government has imposed numerous new and more
intense inspection procedures and protocols that are contributing to the
congestion and delays at the border; and -
WHEREAS, border wait times have more than doubled since 2001 and can
easily exceed more than two hours at anyone of the ports of entry on the Arizona
border with Mexico; and
WHEREAS, port of entry projects can take between seven and 15 years from
concept to completion; and
WHEREAS, Arizona is committed to implementing a plan to at least double the
throughput capacity at all its major ports of entry by 2015; and
WHEREAS, much work remains to be done in order to bring the various projects
to fruition and to secure the necessary funding to pay for each project; and
WHEREAS, Arizona is committed to working with all relevant local, state and
federal partners and in full coordination and collaboration with the state of Sonora
and the federal government of Mexico; and

policy.



WHEREAS, "2015: Arizona and Sonora's New Border" offers a valuable tool for
the information of decision makers, dissemination of information and coordination
of efforts for stakeholders; and
WHEREAS, failure to implement a focused and long-term initiative to address the
border and the ports of entry stands to negatively impact the economic future and
the physical security of the state and the nation.
NOW THEREFORE, the parties agree as follows:
I. Understanding of the parties
1. This MOU sets forth the basis of coordination for all border
infrastructure projects affecting the border region between both states.
2. The parties acknowledge that this MOU does not contain all matters,
terms, conditions, upon which an agreement can be reached to meet
legal requirements that may pertain to final approval.
3. All terms and conditions pertaining to the preparation and signing of a
formal agreement shall be subject to full legal review by mutual legal
counsels.
4. The MOU is effective as of the date set forth above.
II. Intent of the Memorandum of Understanding for State-to-State recognition of
border infrastructure needs
1. It is the intent of the Arizona - Mexico Commission from the Arizona
Governor's Office to facilitate the State of Sonora's process in
generating a similar resolution to coordinate Sonora's border
infrastructure needs.
2. It is the intent of the Arizona-Mexico Commission for the State of
Sonora to develop joint timelines for developing border infrastructure
with the State of Arizona to accomplish the much-needed port of entry
projects within the Arizona-Sonora region.
3. It is the intent that once the State of Sonora presents a similar
resolution, the port of entry projects within Arizona and Sonora will be
executed simultaneously in a timely manner.
III. Status of the parties
Nothing in this MOU shall be construed so as to create a partnership or
agency agreement of reciprocity. The Governor's Office in each state
must meet all federal and state requirements to utilize this resolution
as a tool to give knowledge to decision makers and the dissemination
of information to stakeholders.



IV. Non-discrimination
The MOU for State-to-State recognition of the requirements for "2015:
Arizona and Sonora's New Border" is subject to State and Federal laws
and is terminable by either party with 30 days written notice.
Having read the present Memorandum of Understanding, the parties hereby
agree to its terms and sign it, in the city of Phoenix, Arizona on 2 June of 2008.
