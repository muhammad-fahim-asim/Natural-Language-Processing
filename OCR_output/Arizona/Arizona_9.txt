LETTER OF INTENT
AMONG THE MINISTRY OF ENVIRONMENT AND NATURAL RESOURCES OF THE
UNITED MEXICAN STATES,
THE U.S. FISH AND WILDLIFE SERVICE OF THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA,
THE ARIZONA GAME AND FISH DEPARTMENT AND
THE NEW MEXICO DEPARTMENT OF GAME AND FISH,
FOR THE CONSERVATION, MANAGEMENT, AND RECOVERY
OF THE MEXICAN WOLF (Canis lupus baileyi)
The Ministry of Environment and Natural Resources ofthe United Mexican States, through the National
Commission for Natural Protected Areas, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service of the United States of
America, the Arizona Game and Fish Department and the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish,
hereinafter referred to as "The Participants",
KNOWING that in the early 1970s, the Mexican wolf (Canis lupus baileyi) was extirpated in the United
States, and considered extinct in Mexico a decade later, eventually leading to the development of the
Mexican Wolf Recovery Plan in 1982;
CONSIDERING that between 2015 and 2017, the Mexican Wolf Recovery Plan was revised following
a series of binational workshops to update and analyze the biological information supporting the full
recovery of the Mexican wolfby maintaining a captive breeding program and reestablishing viable, self-
sustaining populations of at least 320 and 200 individuals with appropriate gene diversity, within
historical range in the United States and the United Mexican States, respectively;
CONSIDERING that the goals and objectives of Mexico's Action Program for the Conservation of
Species: Mexican Wolf (PACE Lobo Mexicano) which seeks the establishment of actions for the
conservation and management of Mexican wolf populations and habitat within its historical range that
ensure the success of the species and its self-sustainability within the ecosystem, as well as the
compliance to the commitments established under the Mexican Wolf Recovery Plan taking into account
its genetic and demographic goals for the subspecies;
1



RECOGNIZING that, while the Mexican wolf wild and captive populations are larger now, there are
new ecological, social and financial challenges that must be addressed through renewed and strengthened
inter-institutional strategies that assure the successful recovery of the Mexican wolf;
HIGHLIGHTING the value and importance of the Mexican wolf to the environment and the residents
oft both countries, Mexico and the United States have been working jointly, and individually, to recover
and conserve native species, particularly the Mexican wolf, within its historical distribution on both sides
of the border;
HIGHLIGHTING the value of collaboration to achieve shared goals, "The Participants" have been
working together to manage the binational Mexican WolfSpecies Survival Plan (SSP) Captive Breeding
Program to provide Mexican wolves for release in both countries and to collaborate on the release of
wolves in the United States and Mexico;
TAKING INTO ACCOUNT the provisions and guidelines stated in the Memorandum of
Understanding to establish the Canada/Mexico/United States/ Trilateral Committee for Wildlife and
Ecosystem Conservation and Management, signed on April 09, 1996;
"The Participants" have reached the below understandings and intend to cooperate, in accordance with
their areas of responsibility, current legal mandates, plans and programs of each country:
"The Participants" will elaborate an annual joint Work Plan aimed to carry on specific activities for the
conservation, management and recovery of the Mexican wolf on both sides of the border and foster long-
term partnerships, as well as to strengthen the existing collaboration between Mexico and the United
States in recovering the Mexican wolf.
"The Participants" and their responsible agencies for recovering the Mexican wolf will follow-up the
activities decided upon during the development of the annual Trilateral Committee Meeting for
Management and Conservation of Wildlife and its Ecosystems, and will report the progress and success
of this collaboration at the Shared Species of Common Conservation Concern Working Table of the
Trilateral Committee.
2



This Letter of Intent is not legally binding and does not give rise to any rights or obligations under
international law.
Signed on June 02, 2022, in duplicate, in the English and Spanish languages.
