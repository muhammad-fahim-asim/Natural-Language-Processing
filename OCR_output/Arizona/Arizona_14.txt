MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING BETWEEN THE GOVERNMENT OF
ARIZONA THROUGH THE ARIZONA DEPARTMENT OF ENVIRONMENTAL
QUALITY (ADEQ) AND THE ARIZONA GAME AND FISH DEPARTMENT
(AZGFD), AND THE STATE OF SONORA THROUGH THE ECOLOGY AND
SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT COMMISSION (CEDES IN SPANISH) AND
THE STATE OF SONORA WATER COMMISION (CEA IN SPANISH), FOR THE
DEVELOPMENT AND IMPLEMENTATION OF AN ENVIRONMENTAL
STRATEGIC PLAN IN THE ARIZONA-SONORA MEGAREGION.
The following is a Memorandum of Understanding between ADEQ, AZGFD,
CEDES, and CEA (hereafter referred to as "THE PARTICIPANTS") for the
development and implementation of an environmental strategic plan in the Arizona-
Sonora megaregion. THE PARTICIPANTS are committed to the following:
CONSIDERATIONS
That ADEQ's mission is to protect and enhance public health and the environment
in the State of Arizona by administering its environmental laws and delegated
federal programs to prevent air, water and land pollution and ensure cleanup.
That AZGFD's mission is to conserve the State of Arizona's diverse wildlife
resources and manage them for safe, compatible outdoor recreation opportunities
for current and future generations.
That CEDES aims to develop, implement, and evaluate the State of Sonora's
environmental policies, promote the participation and responsibility of public and
private sectors in the formulation of environmental policies, their application,
information sharing and monitoring, and, in general, the environmental actions
undertaken by the State of Sonora. CEDES also conducts and promotes scientific
and technological research in the fields of ecology and the environment to
encourage and disseminate environmental values and culture in the State of
Sonora.
That CEA aims to participate in the planning, financing, budgeting, and sustainable
development of the State of Sonora's water sector, implementing policies and
actions that establish a holistic water system, advising and assisting water user

That Arizona and Sonora share a number of environmental issues related to water,
air, solid waste, and wildlife which do not conform to political boundaries along their
362 miles of shared border, necessitating that the environmental, water, and
wildlife agencies of both states work together and with their federal and binational
counterparts to achieve sustainable solutions in the region. That the development
of the Arizona-Sonora Environmental Strategic Plan (ASESP) emerged from a long
history of cross-border cooperation involving local, state, federal, binational, and
non-govemnmental organizations and the private sector in Arizona and Sonora.
That in June of 2016, in order to improve cooperation and maximize the effective
use of resources, the Arizona-Mexico Commission's Environment and Water
Committee committed to working on a ASESP for the period of 2017-2021.
That ADEQ, CEDES, and CEA requested support for the coordination of this
endeavor from the Border Environment Cooperation Commission (BECC), and that
other state agencies, such as AZGFD and the Arizona Department of Water
Resources (ADWR), collaborated on specific issues. That binational organizations
such as the North American Development Bank (NADB) and federal agencies such
as the Ministry of the Environment and Natural Resources of Mexico (SEMARNAT)
and the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) were also asked to
participate.
That the plan was designed to incorporate elements of economic and social
development in accordance with the priorities of both states' administrations, for
which the North American Research Partnership (NARP), an independent, non-
profit research organization, contributed its knowledge on economic
competitiveness and the environment in the Arizona-Sonora megaregion (in
addition to organizing, editing, and publishing the ASESP itself).

That this plan was developed through a series of teleconferences, workshops, and
individual consultations, including two multi-agency workshops that were held in
Tucson, Arizona on September 13 and 14, 2016, and in Hermosillo, Sonora on
November 1, 2016. That the purpose of these workshops was to discuss a) the
development and selection of primary projects, b) the criteria for evaluating these
projects, and c) their prioritization, and that this final point was refined over the
course of several teleconferences and meetings between experts from the
participating organizations.
That the ASESP includes background information on: environmental issues in the
Arizona-Sonora megaregion, economic competitiveness and the environment,
priority strategic projects, additional projects should funding sources become
available, and potential partners.
That 15 priority strategic projects were selected that address a number of public
issues related to the areas of water quality, air quality, waste management, and
wildlife management. Another 15 additional projects were selected by participating
agencies as worthy of future consideration as funding and staffing resources
become available.
That the 15 priority strategic projects the agencies commit to further developing
and implementing within the framework of this Memorandum of Understanding are:
Water Quality:
Stormwater Control in Nogales, Sonora for the Protection of Binational
Infrastructure and Public Health
Infrastructure for Metals and E. coli Attenuation in the San Pedro River
Implementation of Green Infrastructure in Nogales, Sonora for the
Protection of Binational Stormwater Quality
Industrial Pretreatment Support in Nogales, Sonora for the Protection of
Binational Water Quality

Therefore, based upon the above considerations, "THE PARTICIPANTS" are
committed to the following:
First.- "THE PARTICIPANTS" will implement the priority projects established in the
ASESP during the period 2017-2021.
Second.- "THE PARTICIPANTS" will report on the progress of the ASESP
implementation through the Environment and Water Committee of the Arizona-
Mexico Commission.
Third.- "THE PARTICIPANTS" agree that the list of priority projects may be
modified through a document signed by "THE PARTICIPANTS" and submitted to
the Environment and Water Committee of the Arizona-Mexico Commission.
Each state will be responsible for its own expenses in the implementation of this
Memorandum of Understanding. Termination of this Memorandum of
Understanding can be made by either state, through written notification directed to
the other state, thirty (30) calendar days in advance of the
termination. Termination of this Memorandum of Understanding should not affect
the completion of cooperation activities initiated while it was in force, unless
otherwise jointly decided in writing by the states.
The States of Arizona and Sonora acknowledge that this Memorandum of
Understanding is only intended to provide for cooperation and a framework for
collaboration in order to advance the tasks outlined herein; it is non-binding and
does not create any legally binding rights or obligations. Nothing in this
Memorandum of Understanding should be read to conflict with the laws and
regulations of the United States of America or the United Mexican States (or any
state or local jurisdiction), or with any relevant international agreements. There are
no third-party beneficiaries to this Memorandum of Understanding.
This Memorandum of Understanding will be effective on the date of its signature
and will be valid through the completion of the "First," "Second," and "Third" steps
outlined above.
