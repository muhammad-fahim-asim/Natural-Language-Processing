MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING
BETWEEN THE SECRETARIAT OF AGRICULTURE, LIVESTOCK, RURAL DEVELOPMENT,
FISHERIES AND FOOD OF THE UNITED MEXICAN STATES, AND THE DEPARTMENT OF
FOOD AND AGRICULTURE OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA,
The Secretariat of Agriculture, Livestock, Rural Development, Fisheries and Food (SAGARPA)
of the United Mexican States, and the Department of Food and Agriculture (CDFA) of the State
of California, hereinafter jointly referred to as the Partners;
WHEREAS the United Mexican States and the State of California share a rich and
common agricultural history that benefits the economies of both states;
WHEREAS SAGARPA promotes the comprehensive development of the
agricultural, livestock and fisheries sectors within Mexico and provides for the
sustainable use of its resources for balance growth among economic regions to
strengthen productivity and competitiveness of agriculture products in new markets;
WHEREAS CDFA serves the citizens of California by promoting and protecting
a safe, healthy food supply, and enhancing local and global agricultural trade, through
efficient management, innovation, and sound science with a commitment to
environmental stewardship;
WHEREAS the importance of the agricultural production, trade, and research is
a priority among the Partners to provide farmers and ranchers in both states the
resources to be sustainable, productive and successful in a global marketplace;
THEREFORE, THE PARTNERS PLEDGE THEIR INTENTION TO:
FIRST PARAGRAPH
OBJECTIVES
The objectives of the present Memorandum of Understanding are establishing the
basis of cooperation between the parties to:
a) Strengthen communication between the Partners to foster greater cooperation and to
adhere to the intent of this Memorandum.

h) Promote cooperation in climate change adaptation for farmers and ranchers.
SECOND PARAGRAPH
AGREED UPON ACTIONS
Further, the partners will put forth their intention through the following agreed upon
actions:
1. Establish an ongoing quarterly conference call between the Partners to include
Departmental leads for the Plant and Animal Divisions and the identified international
coordinator for each respected agency. The conference call will provide a general update
on plant/animal issues and provide a format for greater cooperation.
2. Promote the sharing and exchange of information to better serve agricultural production
and community access to safe, affordable and nutritious food while sustaining natural
resources. CDFA will coordinate with the University of California, Division of Agricultural
and Natural Resources, to host a forum on California's Cooperative Extension Services to
better inform and establish future collaborative connections between California and
Mexico in agricultural extension and research. The forum, among other issues, will
promote the exchange of information in the areas of water conservation, water quality,
invasive pest management, environment and sustainability of irrigated agriculture and
value added areas.
3. Convene a meeting of the California State Board of Food and Agriculture at the San
Ysidro Port of Entry to discuss cross-border agricultural inspection and shipment issues.

The meeting will include representatives from the US/MX Government, agricultural
exporters from Mexico and California as well as trade associations.
4. Facilitate an exchange of information on organic agricultural production and standards to
further trade cooperation between California and Mexico. CDFA in coordination with the
California Certified Organic Farmers (CCOF) will develop an education tour and program
for SAGARPA/SENASICA officials and affiliated trade association representatives. The
education tour and program will review California's organic standards, certification
process/inspection and production practices.
5. Encourage the exchange of information and facilitation of beef/cattle trade between
California and Mexico. Specifically, the development of an efficient and timely protocol that
would facilitate the movement of U.S. raised cattle for harvesting in Mexico. Further,
expanding bi-national cooperation, a pilot program would be developed, for consideration
by federal authorities, allowing for the equivalency in USDA grading for U.S. raised cattle,
harvested in Mexico. Through cooperation of state (Baja California, California) and federal
(USDA and SAGARPA) officials a cooperative joint value chain for beef products can be
developed.
6. Collaborate with responsible state/federal authorities in the development of cross-border
consumer educational materials focusing on important sanitarylphytosanitary issues (such
as bovine tuberculosis, Exotic Newcastle Disease, and Asian Citrus Psyllid) to help
mitigate/detect the spread of invasive pests and diseases. To further this effort the
Partners will jointly host a forum at the Border Governor's Conference in 2015
documenting cross-border consumer educational materials.
7. Organize a tri-national (California, Mexico, Canada) symposium focusing on the issue of
climate change adaptation for farmers and ranchers. The purpose of this symposium is to
strengthen the connection and role that agricultural production has in a changing climate
and support the NAFTA region as a model for agricultural sector work (research and on-
farm implementation) on climate adaptation.
The agreed upon activities and the terms by which those will be executed, shall be determined
by the establishment of working groups agreed upon by the Parties, and These working groups
shall be an integral part of the Memorandum of Understanding to implement agreed upon
activities.

THIRD PARAGRAPH
PARTICIPATING PERSONNEL
Each party shall carry out the necessary procedures with competent authorities, to grant the
necessary authorizations and permits for the entry, stay and exit, of participants working under
official capacity, on agreed upon activities included within this Memorandum of Understanding.
Such participants shall be subject to migratory, fiscal, customs, sanitary and national security
regulations, in force at the receiving country and shall not be authorized to perform activities
different from the original purpose of their visit, without previous authorization of competent
authorities. Participants shall leave the visiting country in conformity with its laws and
regulations in force.
The personnel assigned by each of the parties to carry out agreed upon activities will continue
under the direction of institution to which he or she belongs, so no labor relation is created with
the other, which will not be considered an employer.
FOURTH PARAGRAPH
THIRD PARTY PARTICIPATION
If the Partners deem it helpful or convenient and by their common agreement, individuals and
entities from the private, public, academic, research, and other sectors may be invited to
support the cooperative activities described herein, provided that they can directly and
meaningfully contribute to the achievement of the objectives of this Memorandum of
Understanding.
FIFTH PARAGRAPH
ASSESSMENT
"The Parties" could prepare in a joint manner, reports and evaluations, considering comments
from technical areas participating in the cooperation actions, to assess the progress and
achievements obtained based on this Memorandum of Understanding.
"The Parties" will keep informed their respective international affairs offices, on the progress
achieved at the actions of cooperation to which refers this Memorandum of Understanding.
SIXTH PARAGRAPH
DURATION
Cooperation under this Memorandum of Understanding is effective on the date of its signature.
Unless otherwise extended in writing, this Memorandum of Understanding is applicable for a
period of 4 years.

shall continue to completion, unless otherwise agreed by the Partners.
SEVENTH PARAGRAPH
FINAL PROVISIONS
The Partners acknowledge that this Memorandum of Understanding is not intended to, and
does not create, any legally binding rights or obligations, and will not be enforceable in any
court of law or tribunal of any sort. The Partners declare in good faith to implement this
Memorandum of Understanding to the fullest extent practicable and feasible as determined by
each Partner in its sole discretion.
