MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING BETWEEN THE MINISTRY OF
AGRICULTURE AND RURAL DEVELOPMENT OF THE UNITED MEXICAN STATES
AND THE DEPARTMENT OF FOOD AND AGRICULTURE OF THE STATE OF
CALIFORNIA OF THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA
This Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) is entered into between the Ministry of
Agriculture and Rural Development of the United Mexican States (SADER) and the
Department of Food and Agriculture of the State of California of the United States of
America (CDFA), hereinafter jointly referred to as "the Participants";
WHEREAS the United Mexican States and the State of California share a rich and
common agricultural history that benefits their respective economies;
WHEREAS SADER promotes the comprehensive development of the agricultural,
livestock and fisheries sectors within Mexico and provides for the sustainable use of its
resources for balanced growth among economic regions to strengthen productivity and
competitiveness of agriculture products in new markets;
WHEREAS CDFA serves the citizens of California by promoting and protecting a safe,
healthy food supply, and enhancing local and global agricultural trade, through efficient
management, innovation, and sound science with a commitment to environmental
stewardship;
WHEREAS both Participants recognize the importance of the agricultural production, rural
development, trade, climate adaptation, and research, and it is a priority to both
Participants to provide farmers and ranchers in Mexico and California the resources to be
sustainable, productive and successful in a global marketplace;
Therefore, the Participants have reached the following understanding:
FIRST PARAGRAPH
Objectives
The objectives of the present Memorandum of Understanding are establishing the basis
of cooperation between the Participants to:
a) Strengthen mutual communication to foster greater cooperation and to
adhere to the intent of this Memorandum of Understanding.
b) Promote cooperation and exchange of information on climate change
adaptation for farmers and ranchers.



c) Cooperate on food safety issues; plant and animal health; cross-border
inspections; transportation logistics and trade issues.
d) Promote cooperation in agricultural workforce development
SECOND PARAGRAPH
Activities of Cooperation
Cooperation under this Memorandum of Understanding may be developed through the
following activities:
1. Establish an ongoing quarterly conference call between the Participants to
have ongoing engagement on the topics of food safety, plant and animal
health and other issues of mutual interest to further collaboration and provide
a format for further cooperation.
2. Promote the sharing and exchange of information to better serve agricultural
production and rural communities through community access to safe,
affordable and nutritious food while sustaining natural resources. CDFA will
work to coordinate with the University of California, Division of Agricultural
and Natural Resources and the USDA California Climate Hub to host a
forum at Casa de California to better inform and establish future
collaborative connections between California and Mexico in agricultural
extension, research and climate-smart agriculture.
3. Recognize the need to make agriculture and rural communities part of the
solution to combat the negative impacts of climate change and
environmental degradation, while also addressing the global needs of food
security. This recognition aligns with the policies and initiatives of the
Participants in addressing a changing climate. The Participants are decided
to work together to elevate the importance of climate-smart agriculture within
international discussions and promote further cooperation on this issue. It is
the intent of Participants to encourage 1) Dissemination of Research' to
improve agricultural adaptation on a global scale; 2) 'Farmer Connectivity'
to further international on-farm implementation of adaptation and mitigation
practices; 3) Advances in Agricultural Innovation and Technology' to
improve the sustainability and security of food, farming and the environment.
4. The Participants will endeavor to conduct climate-smart agricultural policy
missions to each other's respective country/state to learn about on-farm
strategies and approaches that are assisting farmers and rural communities



in adapting to climate change. These missions will aim to include farmers,
academics and government officials.
5. To further cooperate in agricultural workforce development, the Participants
will be responsive to the needs of stakeholders in furthering connections and
discussions among agricultural employers, farm workers, non-profits and
local governments as appropriate.
6. Organize a trilateral (California, Mexico, Canada) symposium focusing on
the issue of climate change adaptation for farmers and ranchers. The
purpose of this symposium is to strengthen the connection and role that
agricultural production has in a changing climate and support the USMCA
(Agreement between the United Mexican States, the United States of
America and Canada) region as a model for agricultural sector work
(research and on-farm implementation) on climate adaptation. The venue for
this symposium should be incorporated into an upcoming annual Tri-
National Agricultural Accord Meeting in which the Participants participate.
The activities of cooperation and the terms by which those will be executed, will be
described in the Working Programs decided by the Participants, which will be an integral
part of this Memorandum of Understanding.
THIRD PARAGRAPH
Participating Personnel
Each Participant intends to carry out the necessary procedures with its competent
authorities, in order to grant the necessary facilities for the entry, stay and exit of personnel
participating officially in the activities of cooperation derived from this Memorandum of
Understanding. Such personnel will be subject to the migratory, fiscal, customs, sanitary
and national security regulations in force at the receiving country and will not be authorized
to perform any activity different from their functions relating to the activities of cooperation
without previous authorization from the competent authorities. The personnel will leave
the visiting country in conformity with its laws and regulations in force.
The personnel designated by each of the Participants to carry out the activities of
cooperation under this Memorandum of Understanding will continue under the direction
of institution to which he or she belongs, So no labor relation is created with the other
Participant, which will not be considered an employer.
FOURTH PARAGRAPH
Third Party Participation



If the Participants deem it helpful or convenient and by their common decision, they may
invite individuals and entities from the private, public, academic, research, and other
sectors to support the cooperative activities described herein, provided that they can
directly and meaningfully contribute to the achievement of the objectives of this
Memorandum of Understanding.
FIFTH PARAGRAPH
Assessment Mechanism
The Participants may prepare in a joint manner, reports and evaluations, considering
comments from technical areas participating in the activities of cooperation, to assess the
progress and achievements obtained based on this Memorandum of Understanding.
The Participants intend to inform their respective international affairs offices, on the
progress achieved from the activities of cooperation referred to in this Memorandum of
Understanding.
SIXTH PARAGRAPH
Duration
Cooperation under this Memorandum of Understanding is effective from the date of its
signature. Unless otherwise extended by both Participants decision, in writing, this
Memorandum of Understanding is applicable for a four (4) year period.
Either Participant may discontinue its participation in this Memorandum of Understanding,
through written communication to the other Participant at least thirty (30) calendar days in
advance of the intended termination. Any ongoing cooperative activity approved or
initiated and not yet concluded would continue to completion, unless otherwise decided
by the Participants.
SEVENTH PARAGRAPH
Final Provisions
The Participants acknowledge that this Memorandum of Understanding is not intended to,
and does not, create any legally binding rights or obligations, and will not be enforceable
in any court of law or tribunal of any sort. The Participants declare in good faith their intent
to implement this Memorandum of Understanding to the fullest extent practicable and
feasible as determined by each Participant in its sole discretion.



Signed in Mexico City, on the fourth day of October of two thousand nineteen, in two
original copies, in the Spanish and English languages, both texts being equally valid.
